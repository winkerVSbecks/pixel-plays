# How the ball and fielders work

A ball has two lives. On the way to the bat it follows a formula. Once it is hit, it is stepped as a loose body until something ends it. Distances are world units (read them as metres) and times are seconds. The code is in `src/game/delivery.js`, `ball.js` and `fielding.js`, with the numbers in `constants.js` and `difficulty.js`.

- **The delivery is a formula.** `ballAt(d, t)` gives the ball's position at any time, with no state. Until it pitches, it moves in a straight line from the bowler's hand to the pitching spot, at 15 out of the hand for the fast bowler or 9.5 for the spinner, under a gravity of 12. Its upward speed is chosen so it lands exactly on the spot. Off the pitch it keeps 85% (fast) or 80% (spin) of that speed and heads for its moved line at the stumps, rising so that it is 0.55 (fast) or 0.42 (spin) high when it gets there. A slow ball that comes down again bounces a second time, at 0.45 of the speed it came down with.
- **The hit ball is stepped.** `stepBall(b, dt)` moves the ball in fixed steps of 1/240 s. A ball falling faster than 1.2 bounces: its vertical speed reverses at 0.45, and it keeps 0.8 of its speed along the ground. Slower than that, it rolls, losing 3.2 of speed every second on the paving and 14 in the weeds past the chalk. It bounces off the oil drum, the brick stack and the bowler's brick. Anything standing on the footpath counts as the wall behind it (the house rule: the scooter is the wall). A ball clear above a house front's parapet sails over. Lower, it thumps the wall and comes back at 0.4. Past the chalk (11.6 along x, or 5.9 along y) it has crossed the boundary.
- **Outcomes.** The first wall, chalk line or parapet the ball reaches ends it. Clean over a house front on the full is out (lost in the neighbour's yard). Reaching a wall or the chalk is 6 on the full, or 4 if it bounced or hit something first. A ground shot is worth 4 at most, even if it carries.
- **The chase is planned ahead.** At the hit, `planChase()` replays the same fixed steps ahead of time, up to 8 s, so the plan is exact. At each step it asks whether any fielder could be at the ball yet. A ball on the full is chased after 0.3 s at 4.6 a second, and can be caught within 0.85 of the fielder and below 2.7 (a stretch and a jump). Any other ball is chased after 0.25 s at 5 a second, and gathered within 0.6 and below 1.5. The first fielder who can get there runs to that spot, and the others watch. If nobody can, the nearest one runs to where the ball ends up. If the gather does not happen, the chase is planned again.
- **Catches and drops.** Only a ball that has touched nothing can be caught. A clean grounded shot (timed perfect or good, and not edged) is gathered as if it had bounced, so it can never be caught. A catch taken while the ball is still going faster than 10 is dropped 15% of the time, and then fielded where it fell.
- **Runs.** A fielded ball scores by how far from the bat it was stopped: under 4 is no run, under 6 is 1, under 8.5 is 2, and 3 beyond that.
- **The bowler.** He is the sixth fielder, off balance at the end of his follow-through. He stops only a ball slower than 7. One on the full he takes only where he stands, within 0.4 and below 1.1: he never runs for it. Once it has bounced, he chases it like anyone. From 3★ he can catch one off his own bowling (caught and bowled): on the full, within 0.5 of where he stands, below 1.6 and no faster than 15. At 1★ and 2★ he only ever fields it.
- **Difficulty.** 2★ raises the target from 50–85 to 60–95. 3★ makes catching quicker (fielders react after 0.22 s and run at 5.4), lets the bowler stop a ball within 0.55, below 1.5 and up to 9, turns on caught and bowled, and makes hitting across the movement cost more. 4★ hides the swing cue, and 5★ also hides the movement wedge and moves the ball 1.25 times as far off the pitch.

The widget below uses the 1★ numbers. Pick a grounded or an aerial shot, then set the aim, the speed off the bat and the climb. You see the ball's path on the lane, its height over time against the catch and gather heights, the reach of the fielder who gets there, and the outcome. It is a simplification: there are no props, wall rebounds, scatter or drops, and it uses one field placing.
